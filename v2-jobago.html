The company

Jobago.ai is an AI employment platform that uses large language models to match job seekers with opportunities. The product worked; the open question was distribution. Acquiring users one at a time, direct-to-consumer, is expensive and noisy for a service like employment support.

The hypothesis worth testing: banks already hold a trusted relationship with the exact population that benefits from employment support, and they have real incentives — community reinvestment, customer financial health, retention — to offer it. My engagement was to pressure-test that, end to end, for the US market.

Who we needed to win over

  • Job seekers — the platform's end users, reached through the bank rather than directly.
  • Bank decision-makers — the internal buyers: leaders in community programs, financial wellness, retail banking, and community reinvestment.

The Problem

The strategic risk wasn't the technology — it was channel. Without a credible, repeatable way to reach users, even a strong matching product stalls. Banks were a tempting answer, but "tempting" isn't "validated." The work had to separate the real opportunity from the appealing story: which banks, why they'd say yes, what they'd pay, and how you'd actually reach the person inside the bank who can sign.

The opportunity

If banks are a viable B2B channel, Jobago gets three things in one move: distribution at scale, borrowed trust from an institution the user already relies on, and a clearer revenue model than consumer subscriptions.

Hypotheses

  • Banks represent a viable distribution and revenue channel for employment-support services.
  • Certain bank types — large, regional, or challenger — fit far better than others, and the strategy should target the right segment rather than "banks" in general.
  • Adoption depends on a business model aligned to bank incentives — whether per-user pricing, enterprise licensing, or revenue share.

Validating hypothesis one

Rather than take "banks will pay for this" on faith, I went looking for proof it already happens. I found it: NextJob, a human-coaching outplacement service, already gets banks like Wells Fargo and Fifth Third to pay $1,300–1,500 per borrower for job-placement support tied to mortgage default prevention — a rational trade when a completed foreclosure costs a lender $50,000–60,000 in legal fees and depreciation. That closed the first open question: the channel is fundable.

The second question — where's Jobago's opening — came from reading NextJob's cost structure, not just its success. Human coaching only pencils out on high-value assets like mortgages; a bank can't justify a $1,500 intervention to save a $2,000 credit card balance. That's a structural gap an AI-native platform with near-zero marginal cost can serve — the entire consumer lending portfolio, not just the mortgage book.

Solution — The Bank's Side of the Table

To make the value proposition real, I framed it from the buyer's point of view — what a bank actually gets out of offering this:

"Offer my customers a meaningful benefit beyond financial products — so I can deepen relationships and support their financial health."
Example: employment support folded into the bank's existing customer or community programs.
"Show measurable community impact — so I can support reinvestment goals and the bank's standing in its market."
Example: helping customers find work as a reportable, repeatable program outcome.
"Add the service without building or maintaining it myself — so I can move quickly and keep it off my own roadmap."
Example: a partnership and pricing model that fits how the bank already buys.

Approach

The engagement ran from broad market scanning to a concrete, implementation-ready plan. The deliverables built on each other:

01

Market & competitive analysis

Found direct proof banks already pay for this via NextJob's mortgage-default program (Wells Fargo, Fifth Third) — and identified the underserved segment its human-coaching model can't reach.

02

Segmentation & personas

Ideal bank profiles and the key internal decision-makers to approach inside each.

03

Messaging & value prop

Positioning tuned to bank priorities — community impact, customer health, retention.

04

Business model

Pricing structure options and a revenue model weighed against bank incentives.

05

Outreach & engagement plan

Channels, innovation events, and partnership pathways to actually start conversations.

06

Executive summary

The recommended strategic direction, synthesized into a single decision-ready brief.

Outcome

The engagement gave Jobago what it actually needed before spending real money on a channel: proof banks already pay for outplacement-style services — so the channel is real, not just tempting — a prioritized list of bank types to pursue first, messaging tailored to financial institutions, a recommended pricing and revenue structure, and a concrete outreach plan to start validating in market.
