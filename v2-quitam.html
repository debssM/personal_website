QuiTam Copilot

TL;DR Problem Hypotheses Solution Architecture Impact

TL;DR

An AI tool that helps whistleblower attorneys prepare qui tam lawsuits under the federal False Claims Act — mapping raw, messy evidence onto the legal elements a case actually needs.

Role
Product & Strategy
Timeline
2026 · 24-hr build, ongoing
Domain
Legal · False Claims Act
Stack

Type
Independent project (not employer work)

The False Claims Act lets a private citizen — a "relator" — sue on behalf of the government when they uncover fraud against a federal program. The attorneys who take these cases have to turn raw evidence (billing records, internal emails, invoices) into a structured argument that ties each fact to the legal elements a court needs to see.

QuiTam Copilot does the first pass of that work. You hand it a bundle of documents; it hands back a structured case memo — the entities and dollar amounts, a fraud timeline, an evidence-to-law map, and an estimated damages figure — for an attorney to review and refine.

Who it's for

  • Whistleblower / qui tam attorneys — the primary user, building and filing the case.
  • The relator — the whistleblower who supplies the raw evidence.

The landscape

Adjacent tools exist — Darrow AI focuses on finding new cases, Harvey AI on general legal drafting — but none go deep on FCA complaint preparation specifically, which is exactly the gap this targets.

The Problem

Preparing an FCA case means manually combing through thousands — sometimes millions — of pages to assemble a timeline and connect each piece of evidence to the law. It can take weeks per case, and that prep work is the bottleneck on how many cases a firm can take on. Meanwhile, the market this serves is enormous and growing.

The opportunity

No tool takes raw whistleblower evidence and maps it to the legal elements required to file. The opening: compress weeks of manual case preparation into a structured first draft — and let attorneys spend their time on judgment, not assembly.

Hypotheses

  • If evidence is mapped automatically to the four FCA elements — Falsity, Scienter, Materiality, Causation — an attorney can judge case strength in minutes instead of weeks.
  • A multi-agent architecture will beat a single monolithic prompt, because extraction, timeline-building, legal mapping, and damages each call for different reasoning.
  • The artifact that matters is a structured case memo with citations and a gap analysis — not a chatbot.

Solution — Job Stories

The use cases that defined what the first version had to do:

"Upload a bundle of raw documents and get the key entities and dollar amounts pulled out — so I can stop manually combing billing records for the fraud signal."
Example: flag a clinic that billed the highest-complexity code 92% of the time against a ~40% national average.
"See the fraud laid out as a timeline — so I can show the conduct was sustained and deliberate, not accidental."
Example: "pattern began Jan 2022, was systemic by March, ran through Dec 2023."
"See each piece of evidence mapped to the legal element it supports — so I can judge whether the case actually clears the bar to file."
Example: a strength score for Falsity, Scienter, Materiality, and Causation.
"Get an estimated damages figure — so I can understand the case's scale before investing more time."
Example: a treble-damages estimate against the CMS fee schedule, plus per-claim penalties.

Architecture

A master agent coordinates four specialized agents, each leaving its work on a shared document graph the next can read. The output is a single structured memo — not a conversation.

Agent 01

Entity extractor

Pulls people, billing codes, counts, and dollar amounts from the documents and benchmarks them against national averages to flag anomalies.

Agent 02

Timeline builder

Orders the facts into a chronological story that shows whether the conduct was sustained and deliberate.

Agent 03

FCA mapper

Scores the evidence against Falsity, Scienter, Materiality, and Causation — and checks exclusion lists.

Agent 04

Damage calculator

Estimates treble damages against the fee schedule and adds per-claim civil penalties.

Outcome

The first version was built in a 24-hour hackathon on the Claude API and a multi-agent graph, then validated in conversations with practicing qui tam attorneys. The pitch line that stuck: TurboTax for whistleblower lawsuits — turning a pile of evidence into a court-ready draft in minutes instead of weeks.
